## 1. Independent Weyl-law route

The supplied calculation evaluates, for R=1,

`integral_0^infinity r^3/(1+r^2)^5 dr = (1/2) B(2,3)=1/24`.

With Vol(S3)=2*pi^2 and Vol(S5)=pi^3,

`V_eff=pi^5/12`.

For the 9-dimensional momentum ball,

`Vol(B9)/(2*pi)^9=1/(15120*pi^5)`.

Therefore the leading cumulative count for D physical polarizations is

`N(<=epsilon)=D*epsilon^9/181440`.

With D=256=128 bosonic +128 fermionic physical IIB polarizations,

`N(<=epsilon)=256*epsilon^9/181440`.

The continuous Weyl density is its derivative,

`rho_cont(epsilon)=4/315*epsilon^8`.

The spectrum is discrete with energy step Delta epsilon=1/2. The asymptotic count per occupied level is therefore half the continuous density,

`rho_level(epsilon)~2/315*epsilon^8`.

Do not conflate the continuous Weyl density, the number of states on a discrete half-integer level, and the cumulative count. A15 must be formulated using the cumulative count or an explicitly step-size-corrected level density.

This Weyl derivation is analytically reproducible from the supplied expressions. The assumption that the complete physical IIB field content contributes D=256 is consistent with the 128+128 on-shell polarizations; it does not replace the low-level KRN character audit.

## 5. High-energy coefficient and validity boundary

The Weyl coefficient gives the leading one-particle asymptotic

`z_B(q)+z_F(q)~8!*(4/315)*chi^9`.

For an ordinary thermal trace with equal asymptotic bosonic/fermionic densities,

`ln Z_gas~A*chi^9`,
`A=8!*(4/315)*(1-2^-10)*zeta(10)=512.0087251779...`.

This leading coefficient is insensitive to a finite number of low-lying k=2 corrections. It is analytically derived from the Weyl density and the assumed thermal statistics. It does not validate the finite-temperature partition at chi_HP, where low levels dominate (the peer reports k=2 alone contributes about 64% of z_B and 63% of z_F).

The HO supergravity validity condition is `n << lambda^(1/4)` up to convention factors. At n~N^2 it would require lambda>>N^8, incompatible with the ordinary classical IIB regime `g_s=lambda/(4*pi*N)<<1`, which requires lambda<<N. Therefore the formal gas-collapse coefficient `chi_2~0.43018*N^(1/5)` is not a controlled KK-gas prediction at the collapse point. Keep only the parametric gravitational-collapse scale as a source-level estimate; quarantine the numerical coefficient as an uncontrolled extrapolation.
