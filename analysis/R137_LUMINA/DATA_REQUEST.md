# Lumina data request

**To** · Dr Oliver Zier, Lumina PI · oliver.zier@cfa.harvard.edu

**Subject** · Lumina initial conditions at z = 49 and the electron history behind Fig. 26

Dear Dr Zier and colleagues,

I’m investigating whether an early-universe calculation in Gupta and Samaras’s CCC+TL framework can be compared with Lumina at its starting scale factor, a = 1/50. We would calculate recombination and perturbation growth on our side. Your ΛCDM run gives us a reference with documented baryon and dark-matter initial conditions.

We downloaded your four public histories. The optical-depth table gives Δτ = 0.0390676 from z = 3 to 49. Integrating your fully ionized prescription below z = 3 with the rounded parameters in Table 2 gave us 0.0153034, compared with 0.0153066 in the table. To extend this check, could you point us to two small products, or to an existing equivalent?

1. The CAMB parameter file or versioned settings, and the baryon and CDM density and velocity transfer tables used at z = 49, with gauge, units, normalization and velocity convention. A pointer to the initial ion fractions and the expansion clock used by N-GenIC/AREPO would complete our comparison.
2. The global mass-weighted H II, He II and He III histories or mean proper free-electron density used in Fig. 26. A matched volume-weighted history, if available, would let us reproduce the weighting comparison in Smith et al. We would return a short script and residuals by redshift.

Our interest in conventions arose when a small counterexample in a discrete transport model led Pablo Arrighi to clarify an edge endpoint and which incoming port supplies each bit. We subsequently tested that transport with spectral operators inspired by Connes. Whether those structures admit a cosmological interpretation remains our question to investigate.

Separately, do you recommend a diagnostic for measuring an H I front’s speed relative to the gas, with cadence, cell size and the reduced speed of light accounted for? A method reference would suffice. If a small co-moving patch at successive times is easy to share, we could test the estimator and return its sensitivity to threshold and sampling. Our eventual application concerns a relativistic plasma phase boundary with different microphysics.

Please let us know the access and citation terms for any shared products.

Best regards,
Igor Sabirov
